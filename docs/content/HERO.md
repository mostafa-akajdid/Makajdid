## Hero Purpose

What the Hero must accomplish in the first 5 seconds:

- Tell the visitor who this is.
- Tell the visitor what he does — in one balanced line.
- Give a reason to keep scrolling.
- Leave a first impression that is calm, premium, and human — never generic.

The Hero is the first proof that the portfolio is a product, not a template.

By second five the visitor should know: the name, the role (full-stack), and that there is something worth seeing below.

---

## Visitor Questions

Questions the Hero must answer immediately:

1. Who is this person? — The name.
2. What does he do? — Full-stack development, stated in one line.
3. Is this a real portfolio or a template? — The copy and composition must feel considered, specific, and human.
4. Why should I keep scrolling? — A quiet signal that what follows is real work and real experience, not decoration.
5. Why should I trust him? — The Hero cannot prove this on its own; trust comes from what follows. The Hero must not overpromise, because the rest of the page will be judged against it.

Recruiters (primary audience) must get answers 1 and 2 instantly, with no interaction. Clients (secondary audience) need a reason to believe the person is serious.

---

## Content Hierarchy

From highest importance to lowest:

```
Name
↓
Role
↓
Main message
↓
CTA
↓
Secondary links
```

- **Name** — the identity. Highest visual weight on the page.
- **Role** — what he does, balanced. One line, no qualifiers that tilt toward frontend or backend.
- **Main message** — one calm, human sentence that earns the scroll.
- **CTA** — one clear action toward contact.
- **Secondary links** — professional links (LinkedIn, GitHub, email, resume). Lowest weight.

## Hero Strategy

Based only on the following verified facts.

Verified facts:

- Full-Stack Developer.
- Strong interest in frontend and user experience.
- Usually starts by thinking about the user experience before designing the backend architecture.
- Curious about every stage of software development.
- Wants to present himself as a balanced Full-Stack Developer.
- Does not want to look like a template portfolio.
- Primary audience: recruiters. Secondary audience: clients.

How these facts should influence the Hero:

1. **Balanced positioning.** The role line must say full-stack plainly. No frontend-first or backend-first framing anywhere in the Hero.
2. **Frontend and UX interest as a quality, not a label.** The fact that he thinks about user experience before backend architecture can surface in the supporting message as a way of working ("I start with the user experience, then build the architecture that makes it possible") — never as a "frontend specialist" claim.
3. **Curiosity about every stage.** This supports an end-to-end message: planning, frontend, backend, production. It is the most differentiating verified fact available and should anchor the main message.
4. **Not a template.** The Hero must avoid stock portfolio patterns: generic "Hello, I'm" ladders, overused developer taglines, decorative effects. If it could be bought from a template, reject it.
5. **Recruiters first.** Name, role, and direction must be visible without interaction and within seconds.
6. **Clients second.** The Hero should not sell. Neutral, considered copy is credible to both audiences.

Net effect: the Hero promises, in one line, a full-stack developer who cares about the people using the product and understands the whole path from idea to production.

## Writing Rules

- Human. Calm. Short sentences.
- No buzzwords. No fake confidence. No exaggerated claims.
- No "passionate developer". No "crafting digital experiences". No "building innovative solutions".
- Concrete over adjectives: say what he does, not how impressive it sounds.
- The supporting message may describe a way of working, not a promise of outcomes.
- Every word must justify itself. The Hero is the most-read section; the least-clean sentence is the one that gets remembered.
- One core message maximum. If the Hero needs two ideas to make sense, it has too many.

## Things The Hero Must Avoid

Phrases and patterns that must never appear:

- "Passionate developer" / "passionate about code"
- "Crafting digital experiences"
- "Building innovative solutions"
- "Driving innovation"
- "Cutting-edge"
- "World-class" / "best developer" / "expert in everything"
- "Pixel-perfect" / "I design pixel-perfect interfaces"
- "Turning coffee into code" and any novelty or cliché
- "Welcome to my portfolio"
- "I love building things" used as an opening claim
- Emoji, exclamation marks, or urgency
- Any metric, year count, or achievement that is not verified
- Template patterns: "Hello, I'm Mostafa, and I'm a..." ladders; generic developer taglines
- Anything that claims frontend-only or backend-only specialization

## Hero V2 Visual Direction

Approved design decisions for the Hero V2 iteration. Copy, portrait, and social destinations are unchanged from Approved Content.

1. **Core message as the visual anchor.** The core message becomes the strongest statement after the name — larger than role and body copy, still smaller than the name. The name keeps the highest visual weight on the page.
2. **Portrait support, not competition.** The portrait remains present but is reduced by approximately 10–15% so it supports the message instead of competing with it. Same image, same blob treatment.
3. **Clear spacing.** Increase the visual separation between name and role, role and core message, and core message and the CTA area. Spacing stays tight enough that the hero fits the first viewport on common laptops.
4. **One CTA group.** The primary CTA and the secondary link read as one intentional group: primary carries the weight, secondary stays a quiet text link, and the gap between them is smaller than the gap to the core message.
5. **Social connected to content.** Social links feel connected to the main content block — placed in the rhythm of the text or as a restrained horizontal row — instead of floating independently.
6. **Scroll affordance removed.** The visible "Scroll Down" text and its large mouse-style presentation are gone. At most a subtle icon-only, `aria-hidden` cue remains.
7. **No scroll cue by default.** Prefer no cue at all.
8. **Editorial, human, not a template.** The hero must read as a considered personal site, not a developer-portfolio template.
9. **Core message typography.** Stronger type scale, controlled line length (~2–3 balanced lines on desktop), comfortable line-height, clean wrapping. No quotes, decoration, highlights, or animated words.
10. **Premium from fundamentals.** Typography, spacing, balance, and hierarchy carry the design — not decoration, effects, or ornament.

## Hero V3 Layout Direction

The Hero uses a three-column editorial composition.

Desktop layout:

- **LEFT — vertical social navigation.** Vertical social icons. The social column is part of the Hero composition and belongs to the content: aligned with the content block, closer to the content, not attached to the left edge of the screen, and not floating independently. It should never feel detached.
- **CENTER — main content.** The content remains the visual focus: name, role, core message, and CTA group with generous whitespace and strong hierarchy. The content starts slightly higher than the portrait.
- **RIGHT — portrait.** The portrait supports the composition; it does not dominate it. Same portrait and blob treatment, sized slightly smaller than the original V1 version.

The design should feel closer to an editorial magazine than a landing page.

The approved Hero copy remains unchanged.

Social icons (LEFT column):

- Keep LinkedIn, GitHub, Email, Resume.
- Icons only. No labels, no surrounding cards, no circles, no background, no gray color.
- Solid icons at opacity 1 — black in the light theme — with a subtle hover only.
- Compact spacing, aligned with the content block, closer to the content.

CTA group (CENTER column):

- "Explore My Work" appears BELOW the primary button, not beside it.
- The CTA group remains directly below the core message.

Typography:

- Keep the Hero V2 typography: the name remains the largest element; the core message remains the second strongest element.
- The core message should naturally wrap into 2–3 lines.

Responsive:

- Desktop — three-column layout.
- Tablet — adapt while preserving the editorial composition.
- Mobile — single column: Name, Role, Message, Primary CTA, Secondary CTA, Portrait, Social icons.

Not used in Hero V3:

- No horizontal social row.
- No icons under the CTA.
- No "Scroll Down" affordance.

## Hero V3.1 Visual Balancing

The Hero composition remains unchanged.

Only proportions are refined.

Rules:

- Reduce unnecessary whitespace.
- Strengthen the relationship between Social, Content and Portrait.
- Make the Hero feel intentionally composed.
- The Hero should look premium because of spacing and proportions.
- The Hero should never feel empty.
- The Hero should never feel compressed.

Refinements:

- **Content width** — the content column gains visual presence. The core message breathes while occupying more of the available space, without creating large empty areas.
- **Content position** — the content block sits slightly higher than the portrait (approximately 30–50px) so the Hero grabs attention immediately.
- **Portrait position** — the portrait moves closer to the content; the gap between content and portrait is reduced. The portrait keeps its current size.
- **Social column** — stays vertical and moves closer to the content so it visually belongs to the Hero. Black icons, subtle hover, icons only, no labels.
- **Hero width** — unnecessary horizontal empty space is reduced and the grid rebalanced. The composition feels tighter without becoming crowded — centered and intentional.
- **Role line** — the divider before "Full-Stack Developer" is shortened into a subtle visual accent, keeping the role aligned with the typography.
- **Core message** — wording and typography unchanged; only its composition improves: balanced line breaks, comfortable line-height, natural reading rhythm, approximately 2–3 lines.
- **CTA group** — vertical order kept; spacing tightened slightly so the CTA block feels connected to the message.
- **Visual rhythm** — vertical spacing between Name, Role, Core Message, CTA and Social is reviewed so nothing feels randomly placed.

Design goal: the eye follows Name → Core Message → CTA → Portrait → Social without any element feeling disconnected.
